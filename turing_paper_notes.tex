\section*{Section 2}
What is meant when by a number differing by an integer from the number computed by a circle-free machine (\textit{Computable sequences and numbers} section)? It 
just means that the integer part of the number differs by an integer from any other number computable by a circle-free machine (meaning integers don't matter).

Is it assumed that, after going to a new square on the tape, that square contains no symbol unless otherwise specified? Yes. A machine is always assumed to start 
wtih a tape such that every square on it is blank.

\section*{Section 3}

In the example of the machine that computes the sequence $010101...$, why, after, the configuration determined by the $m$-config $b$ and no symbol, is 
$P0,R$ done, followed by going to the next configuration, which also does $R$. This seems like it is shifting over to places to the right, 
and thus missing one place on the tape (this same question comes up with the third and fourth configurations). This is an example of the \textquote{alternate squares} that 
are being discussed. This is simply something he defines, that each figure in the sequence is meant to be written on alternate squares.

For the second example machine, what is the first configuration, is it the $m$-config $b$ with any symbol or no symbol? Yes.

We should go over the second example machine, as I was having trouble understanding how it really computed the desired sequence.

What exactly did he mean when he was talking about writing figures on \textquote{alternate squares}? 
Does this have something to do with what he said before: \textquote{We also arrange that in the 
sequence of figures on alternate squares there shall be no blanks.} See the answer to the first question in section 3. This is just a convention to leave one square 
between each square as a marker to help in the computation. The other squares are the figures actually forming the continuous sequence.
