Problem 6: Suppose T^ is an immediate extension of T. Show that if T^ is bad, so is T^.

Assume T^ is bad. Thus every branch of T^ is bad, meaning every branch of T^ contains some formula X and its conjugate X~. T was either extened to T^ by rule A or rule B on some branch O to O1. 

Consider the case in which T was extended by rule A. Thus the branch O contains a. Because T^ was assumed to be bad, O1 is bad. O1 must have either a1 or a2 as its members. Because O1 is bad, either C(O, a1) or C(O, a2) is bad. Thus either ~C(O, a1) or ~C(O, a2) is provable, and therefore ~C(O) is provable either by Rule A1 or Rule A2. Because branch O is C(O), it follows that branch O is bad, because ~C(O) is provable. Because all other branches of T^ are also branches of T, and are all bad, the rest of the branches of T are bad, thus making T bad.

Consider the case in which T was extended by rule B. Thus b is member of O. The branch O in T was extended to two branches, O1 and O2 in T^. Because T^ is closed, both O1 and O2 are closed. O1 and O2 are either C(O, b1) or C(O, b2). Becuase they are both closed, both ~C(O, b1) and ~C(O, b2) are provable, thus meaning ~C(O) is provable by Rule B. Because all other branches of T^ are those of T, all other branches of T are bad, thus meaning T is bad. 

Problem 7: Prove that if there is closed tableau T for X, then X is bad. 

Consider the property P(x) for the tableau T(n-x) being bad, where n is the number of levels of the tableau (meaning Tn is just T). We will show P holds for all x <= n by limited induction. For P(0), this is simply the tableau T(n-0), which is just Tn, which we defined to be T, which was assumed to be closed, meaning T is obviously bad, thus proving P(0) holds. Consider some number x less than n. Assume P holds for X. Thus the tableau T(n-x) for X is bad. Well, the tableau of size T(n-(x+1)) are simply the branches on one lower level of the tree than T(n-x). Because all n sized branches of the tree were assumed to be bad, all n-1 branches of the tree are bad by Problem 6 (because all n levels are extensions of some n-1 level of the tableau). Thus, P(x+1) holds, proving P holds for all x <= n, meaning P(n) holds, making T(0) is bad, which of course is just X.

Problem 10(a):

Assume some extension T^ of a tableau T is nice. We must show that T is also nice. 

Consider the case where the branch O of T was extended by Rule B0 to the branch O1 of T^ which is O, b1, or O, b2. Because T^ is nice, O1 is nice. Because O, b1 or O, b2 is nice, O is nice by either rule B1 or rule B2 of U2. Because O is nice, and the fact that all other branches of T are branches of T^ (meaning they are all nice), all branches of T are nice, and thus T is nice as well.

Consider the case where the branch O of T was extended by Rule A0 to the two branches O1 and O2 of T^, which are O, a1, and O, a2. Because O1 and O2 are nice, both O, a1 and O, a2 are nice, meaning D(O) is nice by rule A of U2. Again, because all other branches of T are nice because all other branches of T are those of T^, all branches of T are nice, and thus T itself is nice. 

Problem 10(b):

Consider the property P(x) meaning the dual tableaux T(n-x) is nice. We will show by limited mathematical induction that P(x) holds for all x <= n if the dual tableaux Tn is closed. For the case of P(0), this is just the dual tableaux T(n-0), which of course is just T(n), which was assumed to be closed, meaning Tn is nice, and thus P(0) holds. Assume that P(x) holds for some x < n. We must show P(x+1) also holds. The case of P(x+1) is for the dual tableau T(n-(x+1)), which by definition of the sequence T0, T1,...,T2 has T(n-x) as its immediate extension, and thus by problem 10(a) T(n-(x+1)) is nice as well. Thus, by the principle of limited mathematical induction, P holds for all x <= n.
