\subsection*{Theorem 2.7}
Here is a proof of the classical part of this theorem. Consider some model modal 
$\mathcal{M}$ for which the hypotheses of the problem hold

Atomic case: In this case $X$ is atomic. Thus it must be that $A=X$ and $Y=B$, meaning, because 
we have assumed $A\equiv B$ is valid in $\mathcal{M}$, $X\equiv Y$ (which is the same formula) is also valid in $\mathcal{M}$.

Negation case: In this case $X$ is of the form $\oldneg\phi$. Then either $A=X$ or 
$A$ is a subformula of $\phi$. In the first case, obviously it follows that $X\equiv Y$ is valid in 
$\mathcal{M}$. In the second case, $Y$ must be of the form $\oldneg\psi$ (where $\phi\equiv\psi$ is valid over 
$\mathcal{M}$ by assumption). Then $\phi\supset\psi$ is valid over $\mathcal{M}$ (by assumption), meaning 
$\oldneg\psi\supset\oldneg\phi$ is valid over $\mathcal{M}$. Similarly, 
$\oldneg\phi\supset\oldneg\psi$ is valid over $\mathcal{M}$ (because $\psi\supset\phi$ is valid over $\mathcal{M}$).
Thus $\oldneg\phi\equiv\oldneg\psi$, that is, $X\equiv Y$, is valid over $\mathcal{M}$.

Conjunction case: In this case $X$ is of the form $\phi\wedge\psi$. Then either $A=X$ or 
$A$ is a subformula of either $\phi$ or $\psi$. In the first case, obviously it follwos that 
$X\equiv Y$ is valid in $\mathcal{M}$. In the second case, $Y$ must be of the form 
$\theta\wedge\gamma$, where both $\theta\equiv\phi$ and $\gamma\equiv\psi$ are true in $\mathcal{M}$.
Then $(\phi\wedge\psi)\equiv(\theta\wedge\gamma)$, that is, $X\equiv Y$, being valid in $\mathcal{M}$ follows 
easily.

Existential case: In this case $X$ is of the form $(\exists x)\phi(x)$. Then either $A=X$ or 
$A$ is a subformula of $\phi(x)$. Again, the first case is obvious. In the second case, we show that 
(1) $(\exists x)\phi(x)\supset (\exists x)\psi(x)$ is valid over $\mathcal{M}$. So, for any world $p$ of the 
model, suppose that $(\exists x)\phi(x)$ is true at $p$. Then $\phi(a)$ is true at $p$ for some 
$a\in\mathcal{D}$. It is easy to see that this means $\psi(a)$ msut be true at $p$ (because $A\equiv B$ is assumed to be 
valid over $\mathcal{M}$). Thus $(\exists x)\psi(x)$ is true at $p$. Hence, because (1) is true at all possible worlds of 
$\mathcal{M}$, it follows that it is valid over $\mathcal{M}$. Similar reasoning shows that the converse of 
(1) is valid over $\mathcal{M}$. Hence $(\exists x)\phi(x)\equiv(\exists x)\psi(x)$, that is, 
$X\equiv Y$, is valid over $\mathcal{M}$. 

\subsection*{Exercise 2.1}
(1) $p\vDash (X\vee Y)$ iff $p\vDash\oldneg(\oldneg X\wedge\oldneg Y)$ (by definition of $\vee$) iff 
$p\nvDash (\oldneg X\wedge\oldneg Y)$ iff either $p\nvDash \oldneg X$ or $p\nvDash \oldneg Y$ iff 
either $p\vDash X$ or $p\vDash Y$.

(2) $p\vDash (X\supset Y)$ iff $p\vDash\oldneg(X\wedge\oldneg Y)$ (by definition of $\supset$) iff 
$p\nvDash (X\wedge\oldneg Y)$ iff either $p\nvDash X$ or $p\nvDash\oldneg Y$ iff 
either $p\nvDash X$ or $p\vDash Y$.

(3) $p\vDash (\forall x)\phi(x)$ iff $p\vDash\oldneg(\exists x)\oldneg\phi(x)$ (by definition of $\forall$) iff 
$p\nvDash (\exists x)\oldneg\phi(x)$ iff $p\nvDash\oldneg\phi(d)$ for each $d\in\mathcal{D}$ iff 
$p\nvDash\phi(d)$ for each $d\in\mathcal{D}$.

(4) $p\vDash \Diamond X$ iff $p\vDash\oldneg\Box\oldneg X$ (by definition of $\Diamond$) iff 
$p\nvDash\Box\oldneg X$ iff for some $q\in\mathcal{G}$ with $p\mathcal{R}q$, $q\nvDash\oldneg X$ iff 
for some $q\in\mathcal{G}$ with $p\mathcal{R}q$, $q\vDash X$.

\subsection*{Exercise 2.2}
(1) We first prove the validity of the $K$ axiom over every modal model. Consider some modal model $\mathcal{M}$, and one of its possible 
worlds $p$. We must show that $p\vDash \Box(X\supset Y)\supset (\Box X\supset\Box Y)$. It suffices to show that, if 
$p\vDash\Box(X\supset Y)$, then it follows that $p\vDash (\Box X\supset\Box Y)$. So suppose 
$p\vDash\Box(X\supset Y)$. Then this means that, for every $q\in\mathcal{G}$ with $p\mathcal{R}q$, $q\vDash (X\supset Y)$. It suffices to show that, 
if $p\vDash\Box X$, then it follows that $p\vDash\Box Y$. So suppose that $p\vDash\Box X$. Then, for every $q\in\mathcal{G}$ with 
$p\mathcal{R}q$, $q\vDash X$. But also $q\vDash (X\supset Y)$, meaning $q\vDash Y$. Thus $p\vDash\Box Y$. Hence $p\vDash (\Box X\supset\Box Y)$ holds, 
and consequently, $p\vDash\Box (X\supset Y)\supset (\Box\supset\Box Y)$ also holds, and thus $\Box (X\supset Y)\supset (\Box\supset\Box Y)$ is valid.

(2) We now prove the validity of the Barcan formula over every modal model. Consider some modal model $\mathcal{M}$, and one of its possible worlds 
$p$. We must show that $p\vDash (\forall x)\Box\phi(x)\supset\Box(\forall x)\phi(x)$. It suffices to show that, if 
$p\vDash (\forall x)\Box\phi(x)$, then it follows that $p\vDash\Box(\forall x)\phi(x)$. So suppose that $p\vDash (\forall x)\Box\phi(x)$. 
Thus $p\vDash\Box\phi(d)$ holds for each $d\in\mathcal{D}$, meaning $q\vDash\phi(d)$ holds for each 
$q\in\mathcal{G}$ with $p\mathcal{R}q$. To show that $p\vDash\Box(\forall x)\phi(x)$ holds, we must show that, for every 
$q\in\mathcal{G}$ with $p\mathcal{R}q$, $q\vDash (\forall x)\phi(x)$. So consider some such $q$. We must show that 
$q\vDash\phi(d)$ holds for each $d\in\mathcal{D}$. But we have already shown this. Thus $q\vDash (\forall x)\phi(x)$, meaning 
$p\vDash\Box (\forall x)\phi(x)$, and consequently, the Barcan formula is valid.

\subsection*{Exercise 2.3}
We show that the converse of the Barcan formula, that is, $\Box(\forall x)\phi(x)\supset (\forall x)\Box\phi(x)$, is valid over every 
modal model. Consider any modal model $\mathcal{M}$, and one of its possible worlds $p$. It suffices to show that, if 
$p\vDash\Box(\forall x)\phi(x)$ holds, then $p\vDash (\forall x)\Box\phi(x)$ also holds. So suppose that 
$p\vDash\Box(\forall x)\phi(x)$ holds. Then, for every $q\in\mathcal{G}$ with $p\mathcal{R}q$, 
$q\vDash (\forall x)\phi(x)$ holds, meaning $q\vDash \phi(d)$ holds for each $d\in\mathcal{D}$. To show that 
$p\vDash (\forall x)\Box\phi(x)$ holds, we must show that 
$p\vDash \Box\phi(d)$ holds for each $d\in\mathcal{D}$. So consider some such $d$. Then we must show that 
$q\vDash \phi(d)$ holds for each $q\in\mathcal{G}$ with $p\mathcal{R}q$. So consider some such $q$. But we already know that 
$q\vDash\phi(d)$ holds. Thus $p\vDash\Box\phi(d)$, meaning $p\vdash (\forall x)\Box\phi(x)$, meaning 
$p\vDash \Box(\forall x)\phi(x)\supset (\forall x)\Box\phi(x)$, and thus the converse of the Barcan formula is valid. 

\subsection*{Exercise 2.7}
For this exercise, \textquote{Classical logic} as a reason in the derivation stands for something derived due to the 
assumption that the axiom system we are working in contains the standard rules for classical logic.

(a) \begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
1. & $((X\wedge Y)\supset X)$ & (Classical logic) \\
2. & $\Box((X\wedge Y)\supset X)$ & (1, Necessitation rule) \\
3. & $\Box(X\wedge Y)\supset\Box X$ & (2, K axiom)
